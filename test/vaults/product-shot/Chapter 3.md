The rain had been falling since noon, and by the time Marta reached the crossing the road had given up being a road.

The horse, which had been standing there, bolted.

She did not run after it. There was nothing at the far end worth arriving at in this weather, and the animal knew the way home better than she did.

What she noticed, standing in the ruts with her boots filling, was how quiet it had gone. The rain took the sound out of everything, and the field on the other side of the wall had stopped being a field and become a flat grey thing with a fence in it.

She counted to a hundred, which was what her mother had always done when there was nothing to be done, and then she started walking. The mud came over the tops of her boots at the third step, and after that it stopped mattering.

By the time the roofline came up out of the grey she had stopped thinking about the horse. There was a light in the kitchen window, which meant somebody had got back before her, which meant she would have to explain.
